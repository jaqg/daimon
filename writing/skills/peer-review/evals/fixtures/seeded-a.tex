\begin{abstract}
Layered magnesium silicate films are candidates for high-temperature thermal barrier coatings.
Here we show that our films achieve the lowest thermal conductivity ever reported for any
silicate material, proving that the layered geometry alone suppresses phonon transport across
the whole temperature range. First-principles calculations give lattice thermal conductivities
from 1.4 to 1.9 W m-1 K-1 across the film thicknesses considered, and the layering trend is
consistent across all superstructures examined in this work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Thermal barrier coatings protect metallic components in hot sections of turbines. Current
oxide coatings rely on bulk phonon scattering at grain boundaries, which degrades at high
temperature. Layered silicates offer an alternative because their weak interlayer bonding
introduces additional scattering channels for heat carriers.

In this work we compute lattice thermal conductivity for four layered magnesium silicate
films using density functional theory and the phonon Boltzmann transport equation. We compare
the thickness dependence across superstructures and relate the trend to the interlayer spacing.

\section{Methods}

All electronic structure calculations were performed with the Vienna Ab initio Simulation
Package using a plane-wave cutoff of 520 eV. The generalized gradient approximation was used
for the exchange-correlation functional, and Brillouin-zone sampling used a 6x6x6
Monkhorst-Pack grid. Spin-orbit coupling was included perturbatively for all atoms.

Phonon dispersions were computed on a 4x4x4 q-point mesh with a finite-displacement method.
Lattice thermal conductivity followed the iterative solution of the phonon Boltzmann transport
equation, and results were converged with respect to the q-point mesh density.

\section{Results}

The computed lattice thermal conductivity decreases from 1.9 to 1.4 W m-1 K-1 as the film
thickness grows from 12 to 48 atomic layers. The reduction saturates beyond 36 layers, and the
trend is identical for all four superstructures tested.

These values are comparable to the 1.2 to 2.0 W m-1 K-1 range reported for bulk oxide coatings,
so the layering shifts the conductivity only modestly within that window. The interlayer
spacing correlates with the conductivity across the series, but the effect size remains small.

\section{Conclusions}

Layered magnesium silicate films sustain lattice thermal conductivity in the range of bulk
oxide coatings, and the thickness trend saturates beyond 36 atomic layers. The suppression of
phonon transport is dominated by coherent tunneling of phonons across the silicate layers, a
mechanism that explains the observed thickness trend without invoking grain-boundary scattering.

Future work will extend the calculations to doped films under thermal cycling.
